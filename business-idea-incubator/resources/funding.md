# Funding & Capital Strategy

This resource covers **Funding & Capital Strategy** — understanding pre-revenue funding options, matching funding sources to your startup stage, industry, and value proposition, and walking through every stage of the funding securement process from first dollar to first paying customer.

## Core Principle: No Assumptions About Capital Availability

> **Never assume the founder has a specific amount of capital, savings, or runway.**
> Always ask. Always calibrate advice to their actual financial resources.
> The goal is to help them get from wherever they are to revenue — or to the funding they need — using the most efficient path available.

## Funding Landscape by Stage

### Pre-Revenue Stages (No Income Stream Yet)

These stages are for ideas that have not yet generated any revenue. The goal is to reach **revenue** or **proof of traction** as cheaply and quickly as possible.

#### 1. Idea / Pre-Seed (Pre-Product)

**Financial reality:** No product, no revenue, possibly no team. This is the most capital-constrained stage.

**Funding options, ordered by accessibility:**

| Option | Typical Amount | Who It's For | Effort |
|--------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Bootstrapping / Self-funding** | $0–$5,000 | Founders with minimal savings willing to live frugally | Low |
| **Friends & Family** | $5,000–$50,000 | Founders with trusted personal networks | Medium |
| **Micro-Grants & Competitions** | $1,000–$25,000 | Students, underrepresented founders, specific verticals | Medium |
| **Incubator / Accelerator Stipends** | $5,000–$25,000 + perks | Teams that can pitch and relocate (if required) | Medium–High |
| **Pre-Seed Angel / SAFE Notes** | $25,000–$250,000 | Founders with a compelling narrative and some prototype | High |
| **Crowdfunding (Rewards)** | $1,000–$100,000 | Founders with a community or compelling story | High |
| **Revenue-First (Pre-Sales / Letters of Intent)** | $0–$10,000 | Founders willing to sell before building | High |
| **Government Grants (R&D, Innovation)** | $10,000–$500,000 | Deep tech, cleantech, biotech, social impact | High |

#### 2. Prototype / Pre-Seed (MVP Exists)

**Financial reality:** Working prototype, no paying customers, some validation.

**Funding options, ordered by accessibility:**

| Option | Typical Amount | Who It's For | Effort |
|--------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Pre-Sales / LOIs** | $1,000–$50,000 | Founders who can get 3–10 customers to commit before launch | High |
| **Friends & Family (Round 2)** | $10,000–$100,000 | Founders who need more runway after initial bootstrap | Medium |
| **Micro-Grants & Competitions** | $5,000–$50,000 | Vertical-specific grants now available with a working demo | Medium |
| **Incubator / Accelerator** | $10,000–$125,000 + network | Founders ready to pitch with a demo and traction metrics | Medium–High |
| **Pre-Seed Angel / SAFE** | $50,000–$500,000 | Founders with MVP and early user feedback | High |
| **Revenue-First (Beta Pricing)** | $1,000–$25,000 | Founders willing to charge beta users | Medium |
| **Crowdfunding (Equity)** | $50,000–$500,000 | Consumer-facing products with community appeal | High |
| **Government Innovation Grants** | $20,000–$500,000 | Industry-specific R&D with prototype evidence | High |

#### 3. Early Traction / Seed (Revenue Under $10K MRR)

**Financial reality:** Some revenue, clear unit economics emerging, product-market fit signal.

**Funding options, ordered by accessibility:**

| Option | Typical Amount | Who It's For | Effort |
|--------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Revenue-Based Financing** | $10,000–$250,000 | Founders with $5K+ MRR and growing | Medium |
| **Revenue from Customers (Growth Funded)** | $0–$100,000 | Founders with positive or near-positive unit economics | Low |
| **Friends & Family (Extended)** | $20,000–$200,000 | Founders with traction to show | Medium |
| **Angel Investors (Individual)** | $25,000–$250,000 | Founders with $5K+ MRR and clear growth trajectory | High |
| **Micro-VC / Pre-Seed Fund** | $100,000–$1,000,000 | Founders with $10K+ MRR and strong retention | High |
| **Government Growth Grants** | $50,000–$1,000,000 | Export-ready, hiring-focused, or R&D-intensive businesses | High |
| **Crowdfunding (Equity / Regulation Crowdfunding)** | $50,000–$1,000,000 | Consumer brands with community traction | High |

## Bootstrapping Strategies (No External Funding)

These strategies can be used at any stage but are especially important before or instead of fundraising.

### Revenue-First Bootstrapping

- **Pre-sales / Letters of Intent:** Get customers to commit financially before you build. Even $100 in deposits is proof of demand.
- **Service-Wrap:** Offer consulting or services adjacent to your product idea to fund development. Common: a dev agency building a SaaS on the side, a designer selling design services while building a design tool.
- **Build-Measure-Learn Loops:** Launch an MVP with the absolute minimum features, charge from day one, iterate based on revenue feedback.
- **Community-First:** Build an audience before building the product. Use newsletters, YouTube, Twitter/X to establish authority and gather a waitlist.

### Cost Minimization Strategies

- **No-Code / Low-Code Tools:** Use Bubble, Webflow, Airtable, Notion, Softr, Glide to build and validate without engineering costs.
- **Freemium Infrastructure:** AWS Activate, Google Cloud for Startups, Vercel Pro free tiers, GitHub Copilot for open-source projects.
- **Virtual Teams:** Remote-first, global talent from platforms like Upwork, Toptal, Arc.dev, and Lemon.io.
- **Shared / Coworking Spaces:** If you need a physical presence, use coworking instead of leased offices.

## Crowdfunding Options

### Rewards-Based (Kickstarter, Indiegogo, Crowdfund Me)

- **Best for:** Physical products, consumer goods, creative projects, hardware.
- **Typical raise:** $10K–$500K.
- **What you give:** Product pre-orders, early-bird pricing, exclusive access.
- **What you don't give:** Equity or repayment (but fees ~5–10%).
- **Success factors:** Compelling video, early bird rewards, existing audience, realistic funding goal (typically $50K–$100K for first campaigns).

### Equity Crowdfunding (Wefunder, Republic, SeedInvest, Crowdcube)

- **Best for:** Consumer brands, B2C with community appeal, early-stage companies with some traction.
- **Typical raise:** $100K–$3M.
- **What you give:** Equity (typically 5–15% dilution).
- **What you need:** Formal valuation, financial projections, investor deck.
- **Regulatory note:** In the US, Regulation Crowdfunding allows raises up to $5M; Regulation A+ allows up to $75M with more disclosure.

### Revenue-Based Crowdfunding (Steward, Clearbanc / Clearco)

- **Best for:** E-commerce and DTC brands with existing revenue.
- **How it works:** You give a percentage of future revenue in exchange for capital today.
- **Typical raise:** $10K–$500K.
- **Cost:** Can be expensive if revenue grows quickly (shared upside).

## Angel & Venture Capital

### When to Consider Angels / VCs

**Generally appropriate when:**
- You have at least $10K MRR (seed stage minimum).
- You have clear unit economics or a credible path to them within 12 months.
- The market opportunity is large (>$100M TAM).
- You're building a venture-scale business (10x+ returns expected).
- You have or can build a strong founding team.

**Generally NOT appropriate when:**
- You are pre-revenue and pre-product (unless you are a repeat founder with strong network).
- The business is a lifestyle business (no 10x exit path).
- You haven't validated demand with at least one paying customer.
- You are a solo founder with no track record and no cofounder.

### Angel Investors

- **Who:** High-net-worth individuals investing their own money.
- **Typical check size:** $25K–$250K per investor.
- **How to access:** Warm introductions, founder networks, angel groups, LinkedIn, demo days.
- **What they bring:** Capital + advice + network. Often more patient than VCs.
- **SAFE Note / Convertible Note:** Standard for pre-seed — no valuation set, converts at next priced round.

### Micro-VC / Pre-Seed Funds

- **Who:** Small institutional funds ($10M–$100M fund size).
- **Typical check size:** $250K–$1.5M.
- **How to access:** Warm intro required, strong pitch deck, warm introduction from founder, lawyer, or LP.
- **What they bring:** Capital + structured support + follow-on funding for Series A.

### Venture Capital (Series A+)

- **Typical raise:** $2M–$20M.
- **When:** Clear product-market fit, $50K–$500K+ MRR, growing >10% MoM.
- **How to access:** Investor warm introductions, top-tier accelerator (YC, 500 Startups), strong traction metrics.
- **What they bring:** Large capital + full-stack support + board seat + heavy governance.

## Revenue-First Funding Strategies

These are strategies that generate money before you raise external capital.

### The Revenue-First Ladder

```
Step 1: Pre-Sales (validate demand, get deposits)
    ↓
Step 2: Beta Revenue (charge early users, iterate fast)
    ↓
Step 3: Positive Unit Economics (LTV:CAC > 3x, payback < 6 months)
    ↓
Step 4: Organic Growth (word of mouth, content, SEO)
    ↓
Step 5: Revenue-Based Financing (leverage your MRR for growth capital)
    ↓
Step 6: External Raise (if needed — now you have leverage)
```

### Pre-Sales Tactics

- **Landing page + waitlist with paid upgrade:** Offer early-bird pricing for first 10–50 customers.
- **Concierge MVP:** Manually deliver the service before building software. Charge for it.
- **Letter of Intent (LOI):** Get a signed (non-binding) commitment from a potential customer, even if for a small pilot.
- **Community Pre-Order:** Build an audience first, then offer a product at a discount.

### Freemium / Free Trial → Paid

- **Best for:** SaaS with clear value demonstration.
- **Strategy:** Give away the product to early users, convert the most engaged to paid.
- **Cost:** Hosting, support, time. But zero marketing spend if you're getting organic users.

## Funding Securement Process

### Stage 1: Preparation (1–2 weeks)

1. **Define your ask:** How much? What will you use it for? What milestones will it get you to?
2. **Prepare materials:** One-pager, pitch deck (10–15 slides), financial model (3-year projections), demo.
3. **Build a target list:** Research 20–50 investors who invest in your stage, sector, and geography.
4. **Get warm intros:** Use your network, LinkedIn, founder communities, and accelerators to get introductions.

### Stage 2: Outreach & Qualification (2–4 weeks)

1. **Send one-pager:** Short, compelling summary. Aim for 20–30% response rate.
2. **First call (30 min):** Get to know each other, answer questions, qualify fit.
3. **Follow-up with deck:** Send pitch deck within 24 hours of first call.
4. **Second call (60 min):** Deep dive on business model, market, competition, financials.

### Stage 3: Due Diligence & Negotiation (2–6 weeks)

1. **Investor DD:** They review your metrics, contracts, cap table, financials.
2. **Term sheet negotiation:** Valuation, liquidation preferences, board seats, vesting.
3. **Legal documentation:** SAFE, convertible note, or priced round (Series Seed).
4. **Closing:** Sign documents, receive funds.

### Stage 4: Post-Fund Deployment

1. **Allocate by milestone:** Tie each tranche of funding to a specific milestone.
2. **Track burn rate:** Weekly or bi-weekly cash tracking.
3. **Investor updates:** Monthly or quarterly updates on key metrics, wins, and risks.
4. **Prepare for next round:** Always know your next milestone and how much runway it needs.

## Stage-Matched Funding Recommendations

When advising a founder, match funding options to their specific situation using this framework:

### Decision Tree

```
Is the idea pre-revenue?
├── YES → Is there a prototype or working demo?
│   ├── YES → Are there LOIs or paying beta users?
│   │   ├── YES → Pre-seed angels / micro-VC + government grants
│   │   └── NO → Bootstrapping + grants + competitions + pre-sales
│   └── NO → Is there a clear service-adjacent to fund development?
│   │   ├── YES → Service-wrap / consulting to bootstrap
│   │   └── NO → Micro-grants + competitions + crowdfunding + bootstrapping
└── NO (some revenue) → What's the MRR?
    ├── <$1K MRR → Continue bootstrapping + grants + pre-seed angels
    ├── $1K–$10K MRR → Revenue-based financing + angel + pre-seed fund
    ├── $10K–$100K MRR → Revenue-based financing + seed fund / micro-VC
    └── >$100K MRR → Revenue-based financing + Series A if growth >20% MoM
```

## Common Funding Pitfalls

- **Raising too early** — You'll give away too much equity with little traction. Bootstrap until you have validation.
- **Running out of runway** — Always maintain 6–12 months of runway. Start raising 6 months before you run out.
- **Ignoring non-dilutive funding** — Grants, competitions, and government programs are free money. Always apply.
- **Over-valuing the company** — A high valuation at seed means you need even higher performance at Series A or you'll get down-rounded.
- **Not understanding dilution** — Each round typically dilutes founders 15–25%. Track your cap table from day one.
- **Neglecting cap table hygiene** — Messy equity ownership scares investors. Use Carta, AngelList, or a spreadsheet.
- **Solo founder fundraising** — Investors almost always prefer teams. Get a cofounder before fundraising.
- **Pitching before validating** — Investors fund teams with traction, not just ideas. Get at least a prototype and some user feedback.

## Financial Self-Assessment Questions

When onboarding or returning to a project, always ask the founder about their financial resources. These questions inform all funding recommendations.

### Questions to Ask

1. **"What is your total available capital for this project right now?"**
   - Format: $[amount] or "I have no capital / I'm not sure"
   - This sets the baseline for all recommendations.

2. **"How many months can you sustain this without any income?"**
   - Format: [number] months or "I need income from day one"
   - This determines runway and urgency of revenue generation.

3. **"Can you afford to go 6–12 months without a salary?"**
   - Yes / No / Part-time income needed
   - This affects whether to recommend accelerators, bootstrapping, or revenue-first.

4. **"Do you have access to friends/family capital?"**
   - Yes (amount range) / No / Not comfortable asking
   - This opens or closes the F&F funding option.

5. **"What's your personal financial situation?"**
   - Employed (can work part-time) / Unemployed / Student / Retired
   - This determines whether service-wrap or full-time commitment is viable.

6. **"Do you have any existing debts or financial obligations tied to this project?"**
   - Amount and terms if applicable
   - This affects burn rate calculations and risk tolerance.

7. **"Have you raised any capital previously?"**
   - Yes (amount, stage, investors) / No
   - This affects investor readiness and network.

8. **"What's your target timeline to first revenue?"**
   - Immediate (pre-sales) / 3 months / 6 months / 12+ months / No revenue goal
   - This determines which funding strategies to prioritize.

9. **"What's your ideal outcome — revenue, acquisition, IPO?"**
   - Revenue business / Acquihire / VC-scale exit / Lifestyle
   - This determines whether VCs are even appropriate.

## Funding Readiness Scorecard

Use this scorecard to assess whether the founder is ready for external funding:

| Criterion | Score 0–3 | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Working prototype or MVP | | 3 = live, 2 = demo, 1 = wireframes, 0 = idea only |
| Traction (users, revenue, or LOIs) | | 3 = >$10K MRR or >1K users, 1 = LOIs, 0 = none |
| Clear unit economics | | 3 = LTV:CAC > 3x, 1 = estimated, 0 = unknown |
| Team completeness | | 3 = full team, 2 = 1 cofounder, 1 = solo with track record, 0 = solo first-time |
| Market size | | 3 = $1B+ TAM, 1 = <$100M, 0 = niche/local only |
| Competitive differentiation | | 3 = defensible moat, 1 = incremental improvement |
| Founder network / investor access | | 3 = warm intros available, 0 = none |

**Total score interpretation:**
- **15–21:** Fundraising-ready. Start investor outreach.
- **10–14:** Getting there. Bootstrap + grants + pre-seed angels once you hit 12+.
- **5–9:** Bootstrap aggressively. Focus on revenue and validation.
- **0–4:** Stay employed or service-wrap. Do not raise yet.

## Adaptive Coaching by Financial Stage

When coaching on funding, calibrate your approach based on their financial resources:

| Financial Stage | Coaching Focus | Funding Sources to Emphasize |
|----------------|----------------|------------------------------|
| **No capital, needs income** | Revenue-first, service-wrap, pre-sales | Pre-sales, service-wrap, grants, competitions |
| **<$5K available** | Extreme bootstrapping, no-code, community | Micro-grants, competitions, crowdfunding, pre-sales |
| **$5K–$50K** | Structured bootstrap, early product | Friends & family, micro-grants, accelerator stipends, pre-sales |
| **$50K–$250K** | MVP development, early traction | Bootstrapping + pre-seed angels, accelerator, grants |
| **$250K–$1M** | Scaling, team building | Angels, micro-VC, revenue-based financing, seed |
| **$1M+** | Growth, market expansion | Seed to Series A, VC, venture debt |
